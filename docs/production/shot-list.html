Two shoots, one evening, one tripod. Both replace something currently drawn in code on the website with the real thing. Neither needs a photographer — they need a locked camera and someone who does not touch it between frames.

Shoot A

Colour temperature

Showroom

The website has a slider that moves a room from warm to cool so a customer can feel the difference between a restaurant and a loading dock. Right now that room is an illustration. It should be your showroom — because this is the detail most contractors get wrong, and showing it in your own space is the proof.

Frames
5 — one per colour temperature, identical framing
Temperatures
2700K   3000K   3500K   4000K   5000K
Subject
A corner with something warm in it — wood, stone, a fabric chair. Colour temperature is invisible on a white wall.
Framing
Wide, eye height, straight on. Landscape, roughly 4:3.
People
None in frame.

  1. Kill the daylight

    Shoot after dark, or blind the windows. Any daylight mixing in fights the fixture and the five frames stop being comparable.

  2. Lock the tripod

    Frame it, then do not touch the camera again until all five are shot. Not the zoom, not the angle, not the height.

  3. Lock white balance and exposure

    See the warning below. This is the whole shoot.

  4. Shoot cool to warm

    Change only the lamp or the tuneable setting between frames. Note which temperature each frame is as you go — you will not be able to tell later.

  5. Check before you break down

    Flick between the first and last frame on the screen. If the room does not visibly change colour, something is on auto. Fix it and reshoot before you move the tripod.

The one thing that ruins this shoot

Turn off auto white balance. On auto, the camera decides the room "should" look neutral and corrects the warmth straight back out — so 2700K and 5000K come back looking identical and the entire point is erased. Set white balance to a fixed value (daylight / 5600K is fine) and leave it there.

Lock exposure too, for the same reason: on auto, a dimmer warm frame gets brightened to match and the comparison dies a second time. On a phone: tap the subject, then press and hold until it says AE/AWB LOCK.

Shoot B

Fixture turntable

Any dark room

On the homepage a single fixture turns slowly while the six stages of a project change around it — bought, drawn, supplied, installed, built in, maintained. It is currently drawn in code. A real fixture you actually stock would be better, and it is a lazy susan and a phone.

Frames
72 — 36 positions, shot twice
Pass 1
Fixture off, all 36 positions
Pass 2
Fixture lit, same 36 positions, camera untouched
Rotation
10° per frame. Mark the turntable edge every 10° with tape beforehand.
Subject
One good-looking fixture you stock. A cylinder or spot reads best — it has a visible aperture.
Backdrop
Black or very dark. Light only reads against dark.
Framing
Square-ish, fixture centred with room around it for the beam to fall into.

  1. Mark the turntable

    Tape every 10° around the edge before anything else. Thirty-six marks. This is what makes the two passes line up.

  2. Set up dark

    Black backdrop, one soft light off to the side so the body of the fixture still has shape when it is switched off.

  3. Lock everything

    Tripod, focus, exposure, white balance — all manual, all locked. Same reason as Shoot A.

  4. Pass one, unlit

    Fixture off. Rotate one mark, shoot, repeat, all the way round. Thirty-six frames.

  5. Pass two, lit

    Switch the fixture on. Do not move the camera, the tripod, or the side light. Go round again from the same starting mark.

The one thing that ruins this shoot

Both passes must be the same 36 positions. The website crossfades the off frame into the lit frame so the fixture appears to switch on mid-rotation. If the second pass drifts even slightly, the fixture jumps as it lights.

So: start both passes from the same tape mark, count out loud, and do not adjust the side light between passes.

After

Getting them to me

Format
Straight off the camera is fine. JPEG, largest size. I resize them.
How
Put them in a .zip and send that. A zip lands on disk here; loose photos arrive as pictures I can look at but cannot save.
Editing
None. Do not colour-correct the kelvin frames — the differences between them are the content.
Then
Both swaps are file changes, not code changes. The turntable plays through the same engine that already runs the buildout film.

If either shoot does not happen, nothing breaks. The drawn versions on the site now are finished work, not placeholders — they stay until there is something real to put in their place.

One line to remember

Lock the white balance, lock the exposure,
do not touch the tripod.
