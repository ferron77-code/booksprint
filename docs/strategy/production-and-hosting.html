The Spine

How to shoot the cinematic hero in Higgsfield without creating a credibility problem, what it costs, how the footage reaches the page, and what changes in the technical plan if the site lives on Hostinger.

01

Read this before you spend a credit

There is one way this goes badly, and it has nothing to do with the footage looking good.

The trap

If the homepage hero shows electricians pulling conduit and fixtures going into a ceiling, every visitor reads that as your crew, on your job. That is the reasonable reading, and no disclaimer in the footer undoes it. The brief drew this line itself: AI imagery must never be represented as actual completed work.

The exposure is not theoretical for a licensed contractor bidding commercial work. A property manager who later learns the "job site" on your homepage was generated has a reason to doubt everything else on the page — including the licence numbers and the project list that are real.

So split the job by what the footage is being asked to prove:

Use Higgsfield forWhy it's safe
Atmosphere and light behaviour — dusk falling, fixtures igniting, light raking across a wall, warm pools on a pathReads as mood, not documentation. Nobody thinks a light bloom is a claim.
Non-specific architecture — an anonymous interior volume, a corridor, a facade with no identifiable address or signageDepicts a type of space, not a project you did.
Material and fixture macro — brass, copper, cast housings, lens detail, a transformerProduct-category imagery, the same as a catalogue shot.
Proposed-design visualisations, labelled on the pageExactly the use the brief sanctioned — showing a customer the "after" you are proposing.
Do not generateWhy it backfires
Workers in branded gear, crews on site, hands doing the workReads as documentary evidence of your team.
Anything resembling a real, identifiable propertyImplies a client relationship that does not exist.
Before/after pairs presented as project resultsThis is the portfolio. The portfolio must be real or it is worthless.
Trucks, bucket trucks, signage, uniformsCompany assets. Show the real ones — you own them.
The rule that keeps you clean

Higgsfield can carry the mood. It cannot carry the proof. The hero can be generated atmosphere. Everything below the hero — the portfolio, the case studies, the before/afters, the testimonials — has to be real work, real photographs, real permission. That division costs you nothing visually and removes the entire risk.

And the cheapest footage you own is the footage you are already standing next to. You are on job sites every day with phones in your pockets. Thirty seconds of a real panel swap, a real parking lot at night, a real buildout at framing stage, is worth more to a commercial buyer than any generated shot — because it is the thing they are trying to verify.

02

Higgsfield: the settings that matter

Checked live against your account. Balance is 770 credits, plan Plus.

Use Seedance 2.5

It is the strongest image-to-video model on the account for this job, and it has the two properties this film needs:

  • Both ends pin. It accepts start_image and end_image. The clip is forced to land on your final frame instead of drifting somewhere else and cutting back.
  • Duration up to 30 seconds in one generation. This is the important one — see below.
Shoot it as one take

The usual way to build a 20–25s scroll film is to chain five clips and repair the seams. Don't. A single generation cannot have a seam, because there is no junction to fail. Since Seedance 2.5 goes to 30s, the entire Spine fits in one generation — one start frame, one end frame, one continuous forward move.

Only fall back to chaining if a single long take visibly drifts. Then it is two 10s takes, the second starting from the real extracted last frame of the first — never from the original keyframe.

03

The prompts

One rule decides whether this works

The camera only ever pushes forward. Never let a prompt contain both an inward word (into, through, deeper, push) and an outward one (out, back, pull, retreat) — the clip will reverse on itself. A clip with no direction word at all is worse: the model invents one. If the shot has to change location, scale and lighting all at once, it will cut rather than travel. Here it changes lighting and finish only, along one axis. That is why it holds.

Keyframe A — the start

Cinematic anamorphic still, photorealistic architectural photography.
Interior of an unfinished commercial tenant space at night: bare metal
stud framing, exposed electrical conduit along the ceiling grid,
unfinished drywall, dusty concrete slab. A single portable LED work
light casts one hard cold pool in the foreground; the space falls into
deep shadow toward the back. Strong one-point perspective looking
straight down the length of the space. Deep blacks, faint warm spill
from a distant doorway. No people. No text. Film grain.

Keyframe B — the end (generate with Keyframe A as an image reference)

The same commercial interior, now completely finished and fully lit at
night. Smooth finished walls, clean ceiling with recessed fixtures in
two rows running away from camera, polished floor. Warm 2700K light,
even and architectural; wall-wash uplighting along the base; a bright
warm doorway at the far end leading to another finished room. Identical
camera position, identical one-point perspective, identical lens.
No people. No text. Film grain.

The film — one 20-second generation

One continuous forward dolly, a single unbroken take. The camera pushes
slowly and steadily down the centre of the space and never turns, never
retreats, never cuts. As the camera advances the space transforms around
it: bare stud framing gives way to finished walls, exposed conduit
resolves into a clean ceiling, and recessed fixtures ignite in sequence
ahead of the camera in warm 2700K light. Uplighting rises along the
walls. The far end resolves into a finished, fully lit room with a warm
doorway. Anamorphic, deep blacks, warm tungsten highlights, fine film
grain, slow steady motion. No people. No text. No cuts.

Pass Keyframe A as start_image and Keyframe B as end_image. The pinning is what forces it to actually arrive.

If Seedance drifts

Two alternates on the account also pin both ends: MiniMax H3 (2K output, 4–15s) and FLUX 3 Video (5–20s, 1080p, multi-frame). Try MiniMax at 2K if you want more resolution to grade and crop into.

04

From the MP4 to the page

A scroll-scrubbed hero needs frames the browser can jump between instantly. Two ways, and the hosting choice decides which.

Option A — scrub a re-encoded video Recommended on Hostinger

Keep it as one file, but re-encode with dense keyframes so seeking is instant instead of stuttering:

ffmpeg -i master.mp4 -an -c:v libx264 -crf 21 -g 12 -pix_fmt yuv420p \
  -movflags +faststart hero-scrub.mp4

One request, a few megabytes, no CDN gymnastics. -g 12 is the part that matters — a keyframe every half second.

Option B — a frame sequence Needs a real CDN

Smoother scrubbing, far more files:

ffmpeg -i master.mp4 -vf "fps=20,scale=1100:-2" -q:v 6 frames/f_%04d.jpg

A 20s film at 20fps is 400 frames, roughly 20–30 MB. That is 400 HTTP requests. On shared hosting without a CDN in front, that is slow for exactly the audience you care about. Only take this route with the CDN enabled.

Mobile is a separate decision

Never ship a 20-second scrub to a phone. Mobile gets a poster still, or at most a short muted loop. This is not a nicety — the hero is your Largest Contentful Paint, and Florida commercial buyers are checking you on a phone in a truck.

Either way, the film drops into the swappable hero slot already built into the homepage prototype. That slot is the reason the site does not have to wait on this.

Hostinger: what works, what doesn't

Hostinger will host this site. There are three things worth knowing before you pick a plan.

CapabilityDetail
Serving a static buildFineA compiled site is just files. This is what shared hosting is good at.
Git deploy from GitHubFinehPanel's Git integration handles static and PHP projects.
Git deploy of a Node appNoThe standard Git integration explicitly does not cover Node sites.
Running Node on the serverPlan-gatedNeeds a Business or Cloud plan. Not on entry shared plans.
CDN + US datacenterMust enableAvailable, but you have to choose a US location and switch the CDN on. Default is neither.
The workaround for the Node limitation

You do not need Node on the server. Build the site in GitHub Actions — where Node is free and unlimited — and deploy only the compiled output to Hostinger over FTP/SFTP. The server never runs a build; it serves finished files. This works on the cheapest plan and is how I would do it regardless.

What this changes in the technical plan

Section 15 of the strategy recommended Cloudflare Workers with D1 for forms and the content system. On Hostinger shared hosting that is not available, so:

None of that is a blocker for a portfolio-led marketing site. It is a genuine step down in one place only: the phone-friendly content system I argued for gets harder, and that system is what decides whether the site is still alive in six months.

06

The split I'd actually recommend

You do not have to choose between them.

Best of both, and cheaper

Keep the domain, DNS and email at Hostinger. Serve the site from Cloudflare Pages. Pages is free for static sites, deploys straight from the GitHub repo, builds with Node on their side, and serves from a global edge network — which is the single biggest lever on the Core Web Vitals the SEO plan depends on. Point the DNS records from Hostinger and nothing else changes.

You keep the vendor you wanted, you keep one bill for the domain and mailboxes, and the media-heavy part of the site gets infrastructure built for it.

If you would rather have everything under one roof, all-Hostinger is a legitimate choice and the site will work. Take these three steps and it will be fine:

  1. Pick a US datacenterYour buyers are in Florida. A European datacenter adds latency to every request for no reason.
  2. Turn the CDN onIt is not on by default, and a cinematic site without one is a slow cinematic site.
  3. Build in GitHub Actions, deploy the outputNever build on the server. This also gives you a rollback: the last good build is a commit away.

Order of operations

  1. Generate the two keyframes~20 credits. Get these right before anything moves — the film inherits everything from them.
  2. Draft the film at 720p~130 credits. Judge one thing: does it travel, or does it teleport?
  3. Master at 1080p180 credits, once you have a draft you believe.
  4. Re-encode for scrubbingThe ffmpeg line in section 04. Check it seeks cleanly before building anything around it.
  5. Decide hostingSplit, or all-Hostinger. This determines the form and content-system work, so it wants deciding before the build, not after.
  6. Drop it into the hero slotThe prototype is already built to receive it.
  7. Clear the blocking verificationsLicence numbers, primary phone, service area. Still outstanding, still the thing that stops this going live.
Unchanged and still blocking

None of the above moves without the licence numbers, and Florida requires them in contracting advertising. The prettiest hero in the state does not get the site launched with blank footers.
