Why this is the concept

The audience split was the hardest problem in the strategy — commercial buyers read proof and schedules, residential buyers read photographs. Every solution costs something. Here the clock does the work for free, and the visitor never sees a compromise. It is also, as far as I can find, not something any lighting or electrical contractor has done.

The shoot, precisely

Five or six finished jobs. A phone, a cheap tripod, a piece of tape on the ground so the second visit stands in the same spot. One frame at 2pm, the same frame at 9pm. Nothing else. Without matched pairs there is no Dusk — and with them, this grid is the most persuasive thing on the site.

Signature interaction

The decision
everyone gets wrong

Colour temperature is the choice customers regret most, and nobody explains it. So let them feel it — this is the same room, the same fixtures, the same wattage. Only the Kelvin changes.

2700K Warm White

Restaurants, hotels, landscape, residential. Warm light flatters skin, wood and stone, and it reads as hospitality. This is what almost every home exterior should be — and what most contractors get wrong by installing something colder and cheaper.

Why a jury notices this

It is a tool, not a brochure. It teaches something true in about four seconds, it is drawn from the client's actual expertise, and it makes the palette itself interactive. The same control quietly qualifies the visitor — someone who drags to 5000K is shopping for a warehouse, not a patio.
